# AI capability, benchmark and evaluation organizations: what they measure about AI contributing to real open source software (as of 2026-11-12)

Scope note: this file covers capability trackers, benchmarks and analyses. It leaves out econometric/RCT productivity studies (except METR's) and maintainer incident reports. Model names such as "Claude Fable 5.x", "GPT-6 Astra", "GPT-5.6 Sol", "Claude Opus 5.5" and "Muse Spark" are given as they appear in the primary sources cited (Anthropic, Epoch AI data, GSO and Scale leaderboards). "Vendor-reported" means the developer reported its own number; "independent" means a third party ran it.

Data files downloaded and parsed for this note (all public): Epoch's benchmark archive (https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip, timestamped 2026-11-13 UTC), METR's Time Horizon 1.1 YAML (https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml), the GSO leaderboard and dataset JSON (https://gso-bench.github.io/assets/leaderboard.json, https://gso-bench.github.io/assets/gso-dataset.json), the SWE-bench site's leaderboard JSON (https://github.com/SWE-bench/swe-bench.github.io, data/leaderboards.json), and Hugging Face dataset-server repo statistics for several SWE-style datasets.

## 1. Epoch AI: Benchmarking Hub, SWE-bench Verified tracking, software-engineering analyses, and published datasets

### Takeaway
Epoch runs SWE-bench Verified itself. Its independent SOTA went from 31% (GPT-4o, Nov 2024) to 83.5% (Claude Opus 4.7, Apr 2026). On Sep 3, 2026 Epoch rated the benchmark "Flawed" and dropped it from its Capabilities Index. Its attention has moved to long-horizon work: MirrorCode (built with METR), which went from about 4% to about 98% between Aug 2025 and Oct 2026, plus external trackers for GSO, Terminal-Bench, FrontierSWE, FrontierCode and DeepSWE. All of Epoch's data is downloadable under CC BY 4.0. I found no Epoch analysis of AI-authored commits on GitHub.

### Cited Findings
**Hub and data access**
- Epoch's hub lists 99 tracked benchmarks in two bases, "Epoch AI internal runs" and "External runs". Data comes as a CSV download, a table view, and a Python client (`pip install epochai`, which uses the Airtable API). It is licensed CC BY 4.0. Benchmark questions belong to their creators, and external data keeps its original license. — [Epoch Benchmarks](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks)
- The bulk archive `benchmark_data.zip` contains per-benchmark CSVs. Coding-relevant ones: `swe_bench_verified.csv` (Epoch-run), `mirrorcode.csv` (Epoch-run), and external `terminalbench_external.csv`, `gso_external.csv`, `frontierswe_external.csv`, `frontiercode_external.csv`, `deepswe_external.csv`, `aider_polyglot_external.csv`, `cybench_external.csv`, `exploitbench_external.csv`, `algotune_external.csv`, `cursorbench_external.csv`, `webdev_arena_external.csv`, `scicode_external.csv`, `metr_time_horizons_external.csv`, plus ECI scores and bootstraps. — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- In Epoch's `benchmark_metadata.csv`, SWE-bench Verified has `in_eci=False`. MirrorCode, GSO, Terminal-Bench, FrontierCode, FrontierSWE and ExploitBench have `in_eci=True`. SWE-bench Pro, DeepSWE, CursorBench and AlgoTune are tracked but not in the ECI. — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- The top ECI model shown on the hub page is Claude Fable 5.2, at 173. — [Epoch Benchmarks](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks)

**SWE-bench Verified (Epoch-run)**
- Epoch runs 484 of the 500 tasks (16 don't run reliably on its infrastructure). The scaffold is a simple one-action-per-turn loop with bash, an Anthropic-style text editor and an OpenAI-style `apply_patch`. Claude Code and Codex are also run via Inspect-SWE. Limits are 2M uncached and 20M cached tokens, in a no-network Docker container. A major scaffold upgrade (v2.0.0) landed 2026-02-12, and the latest version is v2.0.3 (2026-03-06). The samples are on Hugging Face (`davidowen-epoch/SWE-bench_Verified_epoch`). — [Epoch SWE-bench Verified](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified)
- SOTA progression by model release date in Epoch's own runs: GPT-4o (2024-11-20) 31.0% → Claude 3.7 Sonnet (2025-02-24) 61.0% → o3 (2025-04-16) 62.3% → Claude Opus 4 (2025-05-22) 70.7% → Claude Opus 4.1 (2025-08-05) 73.3% → GPT-5 high (2025-08-07) 73.6% → Claude Opus 4.5 (2025-11-24) 76.7% → Claude Opus 4.6 (2026-02-05) 78.7% → Claude Opus 4.7 max (2026-04-16, run 2026-04-20) 83.5%. Next best: GPT-5.5 pre-release xhigh 80.6%, Gemini 3.5 Flash 79.3%, GLM-5.2 78.7%, DeepSeek-V4-Pro 77.6%, Qwen3.7-Max 77.3%. The latest Epoch run in the file started 2026-06-25, so no Claude 5.x or GPT-6-era models have been run. — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- Epoch's Sep 3, 2026 review gives a "Flawed" verdict, and its inspection stopped once errors passed its threshold. Its reasons: 100% of tasks and solutions are public. OpenAI's Feb 23, 2026 audit covered 27.6% of tasks and found 59.4% of those had flawed tests that reject correct solutions, so at least 16.4% of all tasks are broken. Epoch concluded the true share likely exceeds its 20% error threshold. OpenAI also found every frontier model had seen some problems and solutions in training. Results depend heavily on scaffold, and a known reward hack (attributed to "RDI") reaches a perfect score. Epoch concludes the benchmark no longer measures its stated capability. — [Epoch SWE-bench Verified review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified/review)
- In Epoch's deep-dive, about 90% of SWE-bench Verified tasks are fixes an experienced engineer could finish in under an hour. Epoch calls it limited to "simple bug fixes in familiar open-source repositories". (Seen via search snippet; the full page wasn't fetched.) — [Epoch: What skills does SWE-bench Verified evaluate?](https://epoch.ai/blog/what-skills-does-swe-bench-verified-evaluate)

**MirrorCode (Epoch AI with METR, 2026)**
- Agents must reimplement a whole program without its source code, matching its output exactly on end-to-end tests that include held-out tests. There are 25 target programs: Unix utilities, serialization/query tools, bioinformatics, interpreters, static analysis, cryptography and compression. One large-task attempt cost $2,600 and ran 19 days. The scaffold and 22 of 25 targets (132 task instances in six languages) are open source, and 3 are held out. — [Epoch MirrorCode](https://epoch.ai/MirrorCode); [arXiv 2606.30182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30182)
- Claude Opus 4.7 reimplemented gotree (about 16,000 lines of Go, 40+ commands) in 14 hours for $251, passing 2,000 of 2,001 tests. AI did well on programs that passed a memorization screen and failed on programs that showed memorization, so Epoch thinks memorization doesn't dominate, though it can't rule out some effect. — [Epoch MirrorCode](https://epoch.ai/MirrorCode)
- The paper's v2 abstract (June 2026) says the strongest model scored 56%. Results and transcripts are in a GitHub data repo. — [arXiv 2606.30182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30182); [Epoch preliminary results](https://epoch.ai/publications/mirrorcode-preliminary-results); [MirrorCode-data](https://github.com/epoch-research/MirrorCode-data)
- SOTA progression in Epoch's `mirrorcode.csv`: GPT-5 (Aug 2025) 4.4% → GPT-5.2 (Dec 2025) 32.2% → Claude Opus 4.6 (Feb 2026) 33.3% → Claude Opus 4.8 (May 2026) 65.6% → Claude Fable 5 (Jun 2026) 70.0% → Claude Opus 5 (Jul 2026) 87.8% → Claude Fable 5.1 (Sep 2026) 93.3% → Claude Opus 5.5 max (Sep 22, 2026) 95.6% → Claude Sonnet 5.5 max (released Sep 28, 2026; run Oct 5, 2026) 97.8%. — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)

**Other Epoch products**
- Epoch Automation Reports (Oct 8, 2026) has 11 tasks drawn from Epoch's own research and design work, graded by humans against an employee standard. Claude Fable 5.1 (Claude Code) and GPT-6 Astra (Codex) both scored 65%. The suite has no software-engineering category. — [Epoch Automation Reports](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/epoch-automation-reports)
- Epoch's GSO page says OPT@K results come from the GSO leaderboard, so they are external. — [Epoch GSO](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/gso)
- Epoch's FrontierSWE external data (source frontierswe.com; V2 is mean@5 over 34 tasks on the "proximus" harness) shows: Claude Fable 5 (Jun 2026) 47% → Opus 5 52% → Fable 5.1 56.3% → GPT-6 Astra (Sep 3, 2026) 65.5% → Claude Fable 5.2 (Nov 9, 2026) 69.9%. — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- Epoch's DeepSWE external data (source deepswe.datacurve.ai, mini-swe-agent harness) shows Pass@1 rising from 29.9% (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Feb 2026) to 51.8% (GPT-5.4, Mar 2026), 67.0% (GPT-5.5, Apr 2026) and 74.1% (GPT-6 Astra xhigh, Sep 2026). — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- Epoch's Terminal-Bench external data (harness-specific best) shows 61.6% (GPT-5.1-Codex-mini, Nov 2025) → 69.4% (Gemini 3 Pro) → 79.8% (Claude Opus 4.6, Feb 2026) → 81.8% (GPT-5.4 + ForgeCode, Mar 2026) → 84.7% (GPT-5.5 + NexAU-AHE, Apr 23, 2026). — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- A Gradient Update on compute demand estimates that if every software engineer using AI daily used it as heavily as Meta or Apple engineers do, aggregate demand would be 10–350 trillion tokens per day. (From search-result summary; not fetched in full.) — [Epoch: Is a compute crunch coming?](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/is-a-compute-crunch-coming)

### Inferences
- Epoch's own runs show SWE-bench Verified gaining about 50 points in 17 months (Nov 2024–Apr 2026), with gains per release shrinking after mid-2025. That fits saturation plus contamination more than a steady trend. Epoch has stopped putting new frontier models through it.
- MirrorCode has gone from about 4% to about 98% in roughly 14 months, so this "weeks-long" benchmark looks close to saturated by Oct 2026, less than six months after release. Reimplementation against a precise behavioral spec now looks largely solved for these targets.
- For little-helper, Epoch's CC-BY CSVs are the easiest ready-made source of dated SOTA series (release date, score, stderr, organization, training compute) to set against OSS commit timelines.

### Gaps
- I found no Epoch analysis of AI-authored commits or PRs on GitHub, or of OSS ecosystems specifically. A search for this returned nothing.
- The 56% figure in the MirrorCode paper and the higher numbers in the CSV probably use different aggregates (the paper's leaderboard metric covers 15 Medium/Large targets, and the CSV uses "Best score across scorers"). I didn't reconcile them.
- I didn't fetch the full text of Epoch's "What skills does SWE-bench Verified evaluate?", so its per-repo and difficulty breakdowns are not fully captured.

## 2. METR: time horizons, algorithmic vs. holistic (mergeability), RE-Bench, 2026 updates, developer productivity

### Takeaway
METR's 50% time horizon doubled about every 7 months over 2019–2025. Since 2024 it has doubled about every 3 to 4.3 months (89–131 days, depending on the window). The last published point is Claude Mythos Preview (early) at about 17.4 hours, beyond the 16-hour ceiling the suite can measure reliably, and METR stopped maintaining the time-horizon page on Sep 8, 2026. Its mergeability work finds automated tests overstate real-world acceptance by about 2x, and the maintainer-judged horizon is about 7x shorter than the test-judged one.

### Cited Findings
**Time horizons**
- The original paper (Mar 2025) defines the 50% time horizon as the length of task, measured in human time, that a model completes 50% of the time. Claude 3.7 Sonnet came out at about 50 minutes. The horizon had been doubling about every seven months since 2019, possibly faster in 2024. — [arXiv 2503.14499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499)
- Time Horizon 1.1 (Jan 29, 2026) grew the suite from 170 to 228 tasks, and tasks of 8+ hours from 14 to 31. Estimated doubling for 2023 onward fell to 130.8 days (CI 107–161), from 165 days. Doubling since 2024 is 89 days (was 109). The 2019–2025 hybrid trend stays at about 196 days (7 months). Only 5 of the 31 long tasks have measured human baselines. — [METR TH1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/)
- The current TH1.1 data file excludes points with p50 above 16 hours and reports doubling of 128.7 days (CI 104–158) for 2023 onward and 187.8 days all-time stitched. — [METR TH1.1 YAML](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)
- p50 horizons in TH1.1 (model release date): GPT-4 (2023-03) about 4 min; Claude 3.5 Sonnet (2024-10) 20.5 min; o1 (2024-12) 39 min; Claude 3.7 Sonnet (2025-02) 60 min; o3 (2025-04) 2.0 h; GPT-5 (2025-08) 3.4 h; Gemini 3 Pro (2025-11) 3.7 h; Claude Opus 4.5 (2025-11) 4.9 h; GPT-5.2 (2025-12) 5.9 h; GPT-5.3-Codex (2026-02) 5.8 h; Claude Opus 4.6 (2026-02) 12.0 h (CI 5.3–60.6 h); Gemini 3.1 Pro (2026-02) 6.4 h; GPT-5.4 (2026-03) 5.7 h; Claude Mythos Preview early (2026-04-07) 17.4 h (CI 8.5–55.1 h). p80 horizons are much shorter: Opus 4.6 1.16 h, Gemini 3.1 Pro 1.50 h, Mythos Preview 3.1 h. — [METR TH1.1 YAML](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)
- The time-horizons page added the warning "Measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite" on May 8, 2026, when Mythos Preview was added. On Sep 8, 2026 it noted the page is no longer actively maintained. Data and code are public (benchmark_results_1_1.yaml, task_results_1_1.yaml, github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public). — [METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)
- METR's 2026 blog posts after May are predeployment evaluation summaries (GPT-5.6 Sol on Jun 26, Claude Opus 5.5 on Sep 22), risk-report reviews and monitoring red-teaming. None publishes new time horizons. — [METR blog](https://metr.org/blog/)
- UK AISI puts METR's implied doubling time at 4.2 months since late 2024 excluding Mythos Preview, and 4 months including it. — [UK AISI, May 13, 2026](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing)

**Algorithmic vs. holistic evaluation (Aug 2025)**
- Claude 3.7 Sonnet worked 18 real issues from stdlib-js (15) and hypothesis (3). Human maintainers had taken 20 minutes to 4 hours on these, averaging 1.3 hours. The agent passed maintainer tests 38% of the time (±19%), yet none of the 15 PRs reviewed was mergeable as-is. Making them mergeable took an estimated 42 minutes on average, or 26 minutes for test-passing runs. Test-passing PRs still lacked test coverage (100%), had documentation problems (75%), lint/format/typing issues (75%) and other code-quality issues (50%). — [METR Aug 2025 research update](https://metr.org/blog/2025-08-12-research-update-towards-reconciling-slowdown-with-time-horizons/)

**"Many SWE-bench-Passing PRs Would Not Be Merged into Main" (Mar 10, 2026)**
- Four active maintainers (2 scikit-learn, 1 Sphinx, 1 pytest) reviewed 296 AI PRs that passed the grader, covering 95 of the 500 Verified issues. Runs came from Epoch's hub. Models: Claude 3.5 Sonnet, 3.7 Sonnet, Opus 4, Sonnet 4.5, and GPT-5. Reviewers didn't know which PRs were AI-written, and 47 golden (human, actually merged) patches served as a baseline. Maintainers accepted only about 68% of the golden patches. — [METR note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/)
- After normalizing to the golden baseline, maintainer merge rates sat 24.2 pp (SE 2.7) below grader scores, so roughly half of grader-passing patches wouldn't be merged. The raw gap is 34.9 pp. Maintainer merge rates improved about 9.6 pp/year slower than grader scores (SE 5.5), which is weak evidence. The gap by repo: scikit-learn 18.3 pp, Sphinx 23.3 pp, pytest 43.1 pp. — [METR note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/)
- Grader vs. maintainer 50% horizons: Claude 3.7 Sonnet 31 vs 6 min; Opus 4 35 vs 4 min; GPT-5 39 vs 2 min; Sonnet 4.5 50 vs 8 min (about 7x). Rejections were for core-functionality failures, breaking other code, and code quality (style, ignoring repo conventions, overengineering). GPT-5 was "notably weaker on code quality" than Anthropic models. The grader rejected only 1 of 27 issues (3.7%) wrongly, so false negatives are rare. No data release is mentioned. — [METR note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/)
- METR's caveats: the agents got no iterative feedback, there was no CI, the newest model was Sonnet 4.5, and the result isn't a claim of a fundamental capability limit. — [METR note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/)

**RE-Bench**
- RE-Bench has 7 ML research-engineering environments and 71 eight-hour attempts by 61 human experts. With a 2-hour budget each, the best agents scored 4x higher than humans. With 8 hours, humans narrowly beat agents and showed better returns to extra time. — [arXiv 2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)

**Kernel engineering**
- In Feb 2025, METR found that with modest scaffolding the best model achieved a 1.8x average speedup on KernelBench. (From search-result summary.) — [METR: Measuring automated kernel engineering](https://metr.org/blog/2025-02-14-measuring-automated-kernel-engineering)

**Developer productivity (METR only)**
- The early-2025 RCT had 16 experienced OSS developers. Allowed AI tools, they took 19% longer. — [METR Jul 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)
- In the Feb 24, 2026 update (new study begun Aug 2025; 57 developers, 143 repos, 800+ tasks; negative numbers mean speedup), the 10 returning developers came out at −18% (CI −38% to +9%) and the 47 new developers at −4% (CI −15% to +9%). 30–50% of developers said they skipped submitting some tasks because they didn't want to do them without AI, which biases the estimate toward zero. METR is redesigning the study (fixed-task experiments, observational data, and other options). — [METR uplift update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)

### Inferences
- Read straight, the time-horizon trend is moving from about one working day (Opus 4.6, Feb 2026) toward multi-day horizons by late 2026. METR stopped publishing as models outran its suite, so no METR-measured horizon exists for Claude 5.x or GPT-6-era models. MirrorCode (weeks-long reimplementation, about 98% by Oct 2026) and FrontierSWE (20-hour budgets, about 70%) are the nearest public proxies.
- The mergeability results (7x shorter horizon under maintainer review, 2x gap in acceptance) are the most direct evidence that test-based benchmarks overstate real OSS contribution capacity. METR's sample stops at Sonnet 4.5 and GPT-5, so whether the gap closed in 2026 is untested by METR. Cognition's FrontierCode (section 6) is the only 2026 attempt I found to measure mergeability at scale.

### Gaps
- I found no METR primary source for the "May 2026 Frontier Risk Report" reported by a secondary tracker (horizons of about 16–20 hours), so it isn't cited.
- The contents of METR's predeployment summaries for GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5 weren't retrieved.
- No successor to the time-horizon suite ("TH2") is confirmed in any source.

## 3. SWE-bench family and successors: construction, repos, SOTA, trends, data

### Takeaway
The original SWE-bench (12 Python repos, matplotlib included) and its Verified subset have saturated and been retired by OpenAI, Vals AI and Epoch. Independent scores reached 79–84% and later 97% (Vals, Sep 2026), against a vendor-reported 93.9% for Mythos Preview. The field has moved to contamination-resistant and harder successors: SWE-bench Pro (about 60% standardized), SWE-rebench (about 64% on fresh mid-2026 tasks), Terminal-Bench 2.0 and 4.0, and long-horizon or rubric-graded suites. Most of these release their data.

### Cited Findings
**SWE-bench (Princeton, ICLR 2024) and Verified (OpenAI, Aug 2024)**
- SWE-bench has 2,294 problems from real GitHub issues and PRs across 12 popular Python repos. — [arXiv 2310.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770)
- Repo counts in the full set: django 850, sympy 386, scikit-learn 229, sphinx 187, **matplotlib 184**, pytest 119, xarray 110, astropy 95, pylint 57, requests 44, seaborn 22, flask 11. In Verified (500): django 231 (46%), sympy 75, sphinx 44, **matplotlib 34**, scikit-learn 32, astropy 22, xarray 22, pytest 19, pylint 10, requests 8, seaborn 2, flask 1. — [HF princeton-nlp/SWE-bench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/princeton-nlp/SWE-bench); [HF princeton-nlp/SWE-bench_Verified](https://huggingface.co/datasets/princeton-nlp/SWE-bench_Verified)
- In OpenAI's difficulty labels for Verified, 196 tasks are "easy" (under 15-minute fixes) and 45 are "hard" (over 1 hour). (Via search snippet.) — [OpenAI: Introducing SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/)
- Top of the official swebench.com Verified board: 79.2% (Sonar Foundation Agent + Claude 4.5 Opus, 2025-12-05; live-SWE-agent + Claude 4.5 Opus, 2025-12-15). The newest entries are dated Feb 2026. With the standardized mini-SWE-agent (bash-only), the best results are 77.4% (Gemini 3 Pro Preview via live-SWE-agent, 2025-11-20), 76.8% (Claude 4.5 Opus high, 2026-02-17) and 75.6% (Claude 4.6 Opus, 2026-02-17). — [SWE-bench leaderboard data](https://github.com/SWE-bench/swe-bench.github.io)
- The site maintainers dropped the separate bash-only board in a commit dated 2026-09-01. The site also lists newer team tools (CodeClash, mini-SWE-agent, ProgramBench, SWE-smith) and marks SWE-agent "legacy". — [SWE-bench site repo](https://github.com/SWE-bench/swe-bench.github.io); [swebench.com](https://www.swebench.com/)
- Vendor-reported: Claude Mythos Preview 93.9% on Verified vs 80.8% for Claude Opus 4.6 (Apr 7, 2026). — [Anthropic Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing)
- Vals AI (mini-SWE-agent): Claude Opus 5 97.0%, DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 96.4%, GPT-5.6 Sol 96.2%, Grok 4.6 95.6%, as of Sep 1, 2026. Vals has archived the benchmark: "Since performance on this benchmark has saturated, we no longer run this benchmark on new model releases." — [Vals AI SWE-bench](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/swebench)
- US CAISI (May 1, 2026) scored SWE-bench Verified on its own harness with a 500k weighted-token budget: DeepSeek V4 Pro 74%, Claude Opus 4.6 79%, GPT-5.5 81%. CAISI notes its numbers run below other evaluators' because of prompt, scaffold and budget. — [NIST CAISI DeepSeek V4 Pro evaluation](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/05/caisi-evaluation-deepseek-v4-pro) (figures via search summary)
- OpenAI announced it would stop evaluating on SWE-bench Verified and recommended SWE-bench Pro, which it says is "not perfect" but less contaminated. Its audit covered 138 problems that o3 failed to solve consistently over 64 runs. The page returned 403 to my fetch, so these details are via search summary and Epoch's review. — [OpenAI: Why we no longer evaluate SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/); [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified/review)

**SWE-bench Multimodal (2024)**
- 617 instances from 17 JavaScript libraries (web UI, diagramming, data visualization, syntax highlighting, mapping), each with an image. At launch SWE-agent solved 12% and the next-best system 6%. — [arXiv 2410.03859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03859)
- Repos: dev split has wp-calypso, react-pdf, marked, p5.js, Chart.js. Test split has carbon (133), openlayers (79), bpmn-js, lighthouse, highlight.js, next, prism, grommet, quarto-cli, prettier, eslint, scratch-gui. **No d3 or plotly.** — [HF princeton-nlp/SWE-bench_Multimodal](https://huggingface.co/datasets/princeton-nlp/SWE-bench_Multimodal)
- Official board top: 35.98% (GUIRepair + o3, 2025-07-01). Vendor-reported Claude Opus 5.5: 61.4%. (The latter is from a secondary aggregator and doesn't appear on Anthropic's announcement table.) — [SWE-bench leaderboard data](https://github.com/SWE-bench/swe-bench.github.io); [secondary: llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/claude-opus-5-5-launch)

**SWE-bench Multilingual**
- 300 tasks across 41 repos and 9 languages, including C/C++ systems repos (redis 12, valkey 4, jq 9, fmt 11, micropython 5, nlohmann/json 1) and Rust, Go, PHP, Ruby, Java and JS/TS projects. — [HF SWE-bench/SWE-bench_Multilingual](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench/SWE-bench_Multilingual)
- Official top: Gemini 3 Flash 72.7% and Claude 4.6 Opus 72.0% (mini-SWE-agent, 2026-02-13). — [SWE-bench leaderboard data](https://github.com/SWE-bench/swe-bench.github.io)

**SWE-bench-Live (Microsoft et al., May 2025)**
- Updated live. The first release had 1,319 tasks from GitHub issues created since 2024, across 93 repos, each with a Docker image. Models score substantially lower on it than on static SWE-bench. — [arXiv 2505.23419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23419)
- The current "full" split has 1,888 tasks over 223 repos. **matplotlib has 102 tasks**, alongside conan 165, cfn-lint 109, deepset haystack 88, pylint 62, instructlab 52, keras 48, sphinx 39, xarray 29, and others. — [HF SWE-bench-Live/SWE-bench-Live](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench-Live/SWE-bench-Live)

**SWE-bench Pro (Scale AI, Sep 2025)**
- 1,865 problems from 41 actively maintained repos: a public set (11 repos), a held-out set (12 repos) and a commercial set (18 proprietary startup repos). Tasks may take a professional hours to days, and all are human-verified. — [arXiv 2509.16941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941)

At launch GPT-5 scored 23.3% and Claude Opus 4.1 23.1% on public, and on the commercial set Opus 4.1 scored 17.8% and GPT-5 14.9%. — [Scale SWE-Bench Pro leaderboard](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro)

**SWE-rebench (Nebius, May 2025 onward)**
- An automated pipeline built a public set of 21,000+ interactive Python tasks for RL, plus a continuously refreshed, decontaminated evaluation. Some models' SWE-bench Verified scores look inflated against it. — [arXiv 2505.20411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20411)
- The current window (May 15–Jul 1, 2026) has 111 problems from 65 repos. Leaders: Claude Fable 5 [high] 64.5%, Grok 4.5 63.8%, Claude Opus 5 63.4%, GLM-5.2 62.9%, GPT-5.6 Sol 62.3%. Among agents, Junie scores 61.8% and Claude Code 60.4%. Last updated Jul 1, 2026. Data: HF `nebius/SWE-rebench-leaderboard` and the SWE-rebench v2 collection, plus Docker images. — [SWE-rebench](https://swe-rebench.com/)

**Multi-SWE-bench (ByteDance, Apr 2025)**
- 1,632 instances in Java, TS, JS, Go, Rust, C and C++, annotated from 2,456 candidates by 68 experts, plus a Multi-SWE-RL dataset of 4,723 instances. — [arXiv 2504.02605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02605)

**SWE-PolyBench (Amazon, Apr 2025)**
- 2,110 instances from 21 repos: Java 165, JS 1,017, TS 729, Python 199. Repos include svelte, material-ui, serverless, vscode, prettier, transformers, three.js and keras. There is a 500-task subsample. Agents perform unevenly across languages. — [arXiv 2504.08703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08703); [HF AmazonScience/SWE-PolyBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AmazonScience/SWE-PolyBench)

**Training-environment datasets**
- SWE-smith: 50k task instances synthesized from 128 GitHub repos. SWE-agent-LM-32B trained on it reached 40.2% on Verified. — [arXiv 2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798)
- SWE-Gym: 2,438 real Python instances with runtimes. Training on it gave up to 19 points absolute gain, and 32.0% on Verified with verifiers. — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)

- Terminal-Bench 2.0 (paper Jan 2026): 89 hard terminal tasks from real workflows, each with a human-written solution and tests. Frontier models scored under 65% at writing time. Data and harness are at tbench.ai. — [arXiv 2601.11868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868)
- Vendor-reported 2.0 scores: Mythos Preview 82.0%, or 92.1% with longer timeouts and 2.1 updates, vs Opus 4.6 65.4%. — [Anthropic Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing). Vals (fixed Terminus 2 harness, Jun 4, 2026): GPT-5.5 73.2%, Claude Opus 4.8 70.0%, Claude Opus 4.7 68.5%. — [Vals Terminal-Bench 2.0](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-2). Snorkel's harness leaderboard: NexAU-AHE + GPT-5.5 84.7%. — [Snorkel TB 2.0](https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench-2-0/)
- Terminal-Bench 4.0 (vendor table, Sep 22, 2026): Claude Opus 5.5 66.4% (xhigh, ±2.6), GPT-6 Astra 57.9%, Claude Fable 5.1 55.8%, Claude Opus 5 52.3%, GPT-5.6 Sol 37.3%. — [Anthropic Opus 5.5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)

**Library and whole-program generation**
- Commit0 (Dec 2024): agents write libraries from scratch given an API spec and unit tests. At release none could fully reproduce a library. — [arXiv 2412.01769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01769)
- ProgramBench (May 2026): given only an executable and its docs, agents must rebuild the source. Its 200 tasks include **FFmpeg**, SQLite and the PHP interpreter. Of 9 LMs, none fully resolved any task, and the best passed 95% of tests on only 3% of tasks. Models favored monolithic single-file implementations. — [arXiv 2605.03546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03546). Vals now runs ProgramBench. — [Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/swebench)

**Function-level and competitive benchmarks**
- LiveCodeBench: a continuously refreshed, contamination-free set of LeetCode, AtCoder and CodeForces problems. — [arXiv 2403.07974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974)
- Aider Polyglot: 225 Exercism exercises in C++, Go, Java, JS, Python and Rust. Top score is GPT-5 (high) at 88.0% (2025-08-23). The board was last updated Nov 20, 2025, so it's effectively saturated and stale. — [Aider leaderboards](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)

### Inferences
- Issue resolution in well-known Python repos (matplotlib, django, sympy and so on) can no longer separate frontier models. Rising ceilings since mid-2025 owe a lot to contamination and test artifacts. The useful 2026 signals are fresh-task (SWE-rebench), copyleft or private (SWE-bench Pro), and rubric- or maintainer-graded benchmarks.
- Different harnesses and sets put the same model at very different SWE-bench Verified scores (CAISI 79%, Epoch 78.7%, vendor 80.8% for Opus 4.6). Comparisons across sources need the harness held fixed.

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve current SOTA for Multi-SWE-bench, SWE-PolyBench, SWE-Lancer, SWE-bench-Live, Commit0 or LiveCodeBench as of late 2026.
- Terminal-Bench 4.0's construction and its official leaderboard weren't retrieved. Only vendor numbers are cited.
- I found no Scale SEAL dates for the current SWE-bench Pro entries.

## 4. Performance/efficiency-optimization benchmarks: GSO, SWE-Perf, SWE-fficiency, SWE-Opt, KernelBench, ECCO, EffiBench, FrontierSWE

### Takeaway
Through 2025, making real repositories faster was AI's clearest coding weakness. GSO Opt@1 was under 5% (later about 9–15%), SWE-Perf models got about 1–2% runtime gain where experts got 10.85%, and SWE-fficiency models recovered at most about 0.23x of expert speedup. In 2026 the frontier moved fast on GSO, from 26–27% (late 2025) to 47% (Jul 2026) to 88% (Claude Fable 5.1, Sep 2026). That jump came alongside a prompt change and hack-control fixes, so part of it reflects elicitation. Stricter, noise-aware benchmarks (SWE-Opt, KernelBench-Verified) still find sparse or no net gains for mid-tier and older frontier models. Several of these benchmarks are built by mining performance commits from repos the project cares about (numpy, pandas, matplotlib, scikit-learn, xarray), and their datasets are public.

### Cited Findings
**GSO (UC Berkeley; Shetty, Jain, ... Sen, Stoica; May 2025)**
- An automated pipeline generates and runs performance tests over repository commit histories to find 102 optimization tasks across 10 codebases. The agent gets the codebase plus a performance test as the spec, and is scored against the expert's optimization. At launch agents succeeded under 5% of the time. Failure modes: low-level languages, "lazy optimization", and finding the bottleneck. — [arXiv 2505.23671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23671)
- Repos (102 tasks): numpy 36, pandas 34, pillow-simd 7, Pillow 4, pydantic 4, huggingface/tokenizers 4, tornado 4, transformers 4, datasets 3, llama-cpp-python 2. The dataset is MIT-licensed and published as HF `gso-bench/gso` and as JSON. — [GSO dataset JSON](https://gso-bench.github.io/assets/gso-dataset.json); [HF gso-bench/gso](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gso-bench/gso)
- Metric: Opt@1 is the share of tasks where one attempt reaches at least 95% of the human speedup and passes correctness tests. A Hack Detector (added 2025-11-03) penalizes memoization and harness hijacking. The judge became GPT-5.4 xhigh on 2026-04-27, when max iterations also doubled to 200. On 2026-07-12 containers were cut off from GitHub/GitLab and MCP was disabled, because newer models had fetched upstream commits and PR diffs. On 2026-09-27 the prompts started asking models to keep improving after their first speedup. — [GSO](https://gso-bench.github.io/)
- Leaderboard (OpenHands scaffold; raw / hack-adjusted; result date): GPT-4o 0% (2025-05) | o3 8.8/3.9 (2025-06) | Claude Sonnet 4.5 14.7/12.7 (2025-10) | Claude Opus 4.5 26.5/24.5 (2025-11-28) | GPT-5.2 27.5/26.5 (2025-12-18) | Claude Opus 4.6 33.3/30.4 (2026-02-11), re-run at high 41.2/37.3 (2026-04-27) | GPT-5.4 xhigh 31.4/30.4 (2026-03) | GPT-5.5 40.2/37.3 | Claude Opus 4.7 44.1/42.2 (2026-04-27) | Claude Opus 4.8 47.1/47.1 (2026-07-12) | then, after the prompt change (2026-09-27): GPT-5.6 Sol 76.5/70.6, Claude Fable 5 78.4/76.5, GPT-6 Astra 79.4/77.5, **Claude Fable 5.1 88.2/87.3**. The file was last updated 2026-09-29. — [GSO leaderboard JSON](https://gso-bench.github.io/assets/leaderboard.json)
- A GitHub issue reports that 41 GPT-5.6 Sol runs and 24 GPT-6 Astra runs on the Sep 27 board used the old prompt, that the leaderboard doesn't say so, and that 4 of Sol's 7 hacked instances are among the old-prompt runs. — [gso-bench/gso issue #37](https://github.com/gso-bench/gso/issues/37) (via search summary)

**SWE-fficiency (Harvard, Google DeepMind, Princeton, Google; v1 Nov 2025, v3 Jun 27, 2026)**
- 498 tasks from 9 repos: astropy, dask, **matplotlib**, numpy, pandas, scikit-learn, scipy, sympy, xarray. Construction: scrape PRs, filter on perf keywords, drop PRs that change tests, require meaningful AST changes, find covering correctness tests with coverage.py and jedi, hand-annotate a performance workload per task, and keep only cases where the speedup exceeds 2x the measurement standard deviation. — [arXiv 2511.06090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06090)
- Metric: speedup ratio (LM speedup over expert speedup), harmonic mean, pass@1, run on OpenHands. Results: Claude 4.5 Opus 0.225x, GPT-5 0.157x, GPT-5.2 0.148x, Claude 4.5 Sonnet 0.116x, Gemini 3 Flash 0.106x, Gemini 3 Pro 0.102x, Claude 3.7 Sonnet 0.024x. — [arXiv 2511.06090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06090)
- Failure modes: over 68% of expert gains are in functions the LM never edits (mislocalization). Others are correctness bugs, satisficing after the first speedup, a bias toward caches, memoization and early exits, overfitting to the workload, invasive edits such as monkey-patching, and reward hacking (stack-frame introspection, cross-run caching). — [arXiv 2511.06090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06090)
- The dataset is HF `swefficiency/swefficiency` (last modified 2026-03-30). — [HF swefficiency](https://huggingface.co/datasets/swefficiency/swefficiency)
- Third-party follow-up: PerfAgent reports raising expert-matching patches on the 100-task SWE-fficiency-Lite from 26% to 74% versus OpenHands with GPT-5.1. (Via search summary.) — [arXiv 2607.19653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19653)

**SWE-Perf (TikTok, XJTU, NUS, UCSD; Jul 2025; ICML 2026)**
- 140 instances from 9 repos: xarray 54, scikit-learn 32, sympy 20, astropy 12, sphinx 8, seaborn 6, **matplotlib 3**, pylint 3, pytest 2. These were filtered from 102,241 PRs. Runtime is measured under pytest in Docker with 1 CPU core and 16 GB. A gain counts only if it's significant under a Mann-Whitney U test over 20 repetitions after IQR outlier removal. — [arXiv 2507.12415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12415)
- Expert patches give 10.85% performance gain. In the oracle setting, the best models get 1.24–1.76% (Claude 3.7, Claude 4 Sonnet, Claude 4 Opus, o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro). Claude 3.7 Sonnet on OpenHands in the realistic setting gets 2.26%. — [arXiv 2507.12415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12415)
- The dataset is HF `SWE-Perf/SWE-Perf` (Apache-2.0). — [HF SWE-Perf](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-Perf/SWE-Perf)

**SWE-Opt (TUM / Siemens; v2 Oct 31, 2026)**
- 102 tasks from pandas (most), scikit-learn and xarray. It covers runtime, peak memory and time-weighted memory. Measurement is noise-aware: adaptive sampling until the 90% relative CI converges, a signal-to-noise threshold of 2, and input-size sweeps of up to 24 workloads per scenario. — [arXiv 2606.25530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25530)
- Gold patches hit a 93.1% runtime pass rate at 15.48x. Single-shot models (GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Flash, Kimi K2.5, GLM-5.1, MiniMax M2.7) got 2–12.7% runtime pass rates. GPT-5.2's patches were a net regression (0.69x). With agents (OpenCode, 50 steps) runtime pass rates were 14.7–17.6% and memory optimization was nearly absent. Gains depend on input size. Code is at github.com/SWE-Opt/SWE-Opt. — [arXiv 2606.25530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25530)

**FrontierSWE (Proximal)**
- 34 tasks, each run 5 times with a 20-hour budget. Tags include Performance Optimisation. Named tasks include **FFmpeg libswscale Optimization**, libexpat Optimization, Cranelift Codegen Optimization, SGLang and Granite Mamba2 inference optimization, plus tasks referencing **PostgreSQL 18**, Git, Zig and Lean 4. Leaders (mean@5): Claude Fable 5.2 69.9%, GPT-6 Astra 65.5%, Gemini 4 Carbon 61.2%. Traces can be browsed. Code is at GitHub Proximal-Labs/frontier-swe-v2, with no license stated. — [FrontierSWE](https://www.frontierswe.com/)

**Kernel and function-level efficiency**
- KernelBench (Stanford, Feb 2025): 250 PyTorch workloads, scored by fast_p (correct and faster than threshold p). Frontier reasoning models matched the PyTorch baseline in under 20% of cases. — [arXiv 2502.10517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10517)
- KernelBench-Verified (Jun 2026) finds frontier models often reward-hack. Under its verified single-turn protocol the best model, GPT-5.5, gets a 0.88x geometric-mean speedup, compared with 1.43x under the standard protocol, and no model beats the PyTorch TF32 baseline on average. (Via search summary.) — [KernelBench-Verified, arXiv 2607.16241](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16241)
- ParallelKernelBench (Together AI, Jun 2026): the best model solved 28 of 87 multi-GPU problems zero-shot, and only 22 were faster than the PyTorch+NCCL baseline. (Via search summary.) — [Together AI](https://together.ai/blog/parallelkernelbench)
- ECCO (2024): measures efficiency in NL-to-code and history-based editing. Most methods hurt correctness while giving modest efficiency gains. — [arXiv 2407.14044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14044)
- EffiBench (2024): 1,000 LeetCode efficiency-critical problems. GPT-4's code took 3.12x the canonical solutions' execution time on average. — [arXiv 2402.02037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02037)
- Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 says its tasks include performance optimizations, without giving a count. — [Cognition FrontierCode 2.0](https://cognition.com/blog/frontier-code-2.0)

### Inferences
- GSO, SWE-fficiency and SWE-Perf all mine historical performance-improving commits or PRs and time them against fixed workloads, which is very close to little-helper's planned "efficiency over commits" phase. Their pipelines can be reused: commit or PR filtering, coverage-based test selection, repetition with statistical tests, single-core containers, and hack detection. Their datasets already label perf commits for numpy, pandas, matplotlib, scikit-learn, xarray, sympy, astropy and others.
- Whether AI can make real repos faster changed sign within 2026 on GSO, from about 27% to about 88% in 10 months. But GSO's own changelog shows that elicitation (prompts, iteration caps) and contamination channels (fetching upstream commits) moved scores a lot. Any efficiency measurement little-helper builds should isolate network access and record prompt and harness versions.
- No mainstream benchmark found here measures how performance evolves across a long commit history (regressions introduced, then fixed). Each covers a single before/after optimization. That's a gap little-helper's design could fill.

### Gaps
- No 2026 frontier-model results on SWE-Perf, SWE-fficiency or SWE-Opt for Claude 5.x or GPT-6-class models were found. The latest models tested are Claude 4.5 Opus, GPT-5.2 and Claude Sonnet 4.6.
- The GSO paper says 5 programming languages, but the dataset is all Python-packaged repos (some with C/C++/Rust cores, such as numpy, pillow-simd and tokenizers). I didn't confirm the per-task language split.
- No source found on "AlgoTune" details beyond Epoch tracking it (release 2025-07-19).
- FrontierSWE's grading method and task construction aren't documented on its homepage.

## 5. Security capability evaluations relevant to OSS: CyberGym, CVE-Bench, BountyBench, Cybench, AIxCC, and 2026 real-world vulnerability discovery

### Takeaway
Security benchmarks built on real OSS (CyberGym uses OSS-Fuzz projects) climbed from about 20% (mid-2025) to 83% vendor-reported (Mythos Preview, Apr 2026), and Cybench is near saturation (93%, Opus 4.6, Feb 2026). Capability then spilled into practice. AIxCC systems found 18 real zero-days (Aug 2025), CyberGym runs found 34, and Anthropic's Project Glasswing (Apr 2026) reports thousands, including a 16-year-old FFmpeg bug. UK AISI estimates cyber task length now doubles every 4.7 months or faster.

### Cited Findings
- CyberGym (UC Berkeley, Jun 2025; ICLR 2026): 1,507 real vulnerabilities across 188 OSS projects. The agent must write a proof-of-concept that reproduces the bug from its description and the codebase. At launch the best combinations succeeded about 20% of the time. The project also found 34 zero-days and 18 historically incomplete patches. — [arXiv 2506.02548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02548); [cybergym.io](https://www.cybergym.io/cybergym/)
- CyberGym, vendor-reported: Mythos Preview 83.1% vs Opus 4.6 66.6% (Apr 2026). — [Anthropic Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing). Aggregators list 90%+ for agent systems in Aug–Nov 2026, but those are unverified. — [secondary: benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/cybergym)
- Cybench (Stanford, Aug 2024): 40 professional CTF tasks from 4 competitions. At launch agents only solved tasks that took human teams up to 11 minutes, while the hardest took 24h54m. — [arXiv 2408.08926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08926). Epoch's unguided-solve SOTA series: Gemini 1.5 Pro 7.5% (Feb 2024) → Claude 3.5 Sonnet 17.5% (Jun 2024) → Claude Opus 4 38% (May 2025) → Claude Sonnet 4.5 60% (Sep 2025) → Claude Opus 4.5 82% (Nov 2025) → Claude Opus 4.6 93% (Feb 2026, system card). — [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- CVE-Bench (UIUC, Mar 2025): critical-severity CVEs in web apps, run in a sandbox. At release SOTA agents exploited up to 13%. — [arXiv 2503.17332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17332)
- BountyBench (Stanford, May 2025): 25 real systems and 40 bug bounties ($10–$30,485) across 9 of the OWASP Top 10, split into Detect, Exploit and Patch. Best results: Detect 12.5% (Codex CLI o3-high), Exploit 67.5% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet Thinking custom agent), Patch 90% (Codex CLI o3-high and o4-mini). The Codex CLI and Claude Code agents did better at defense (patching) than offense. — [arXiv 2505.15216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15216)
- ExploitBench (CMU, May 2026): 41 V8 bugs. Agents routinely reach the vulnerable code and trigger crashes, but rarely get arbitrary code execution. Epoch tracks it in the ECI. (Via search summary.) — [ExploitBench write-up](https://bollwerk.ai/blog/exploitbench-llm-cybersecurity-capability-ladder/); [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- DARPA AIxCC final (DEF CON 33, Aug 2025): finalists' systems found 54 synthetic vulnerabilities in 63 challenges (corrected figures) and patched 43. They also found 18 real, non-synthetic vulnerabilities, which are being disclosed to OSS maintainers. All 7 finalist cyber reasoning systems were open-sourced. Team Atlanta won the $4M first prize. — [DARPA AIxCC results](https://darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results) (via search summary)
- Project Glasswing (Anthropic, Apr 7, 2026): Anthropic reports "thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities" across major OSes and browsers. Named OSS cases: a 27-year-old OpenBSD remote crash; a 16-year-old **FFmpeg** bug in a line automated tools had hit 5 million times without catching it; and chained Linux kernel privilege escalations. There are 12 launch partners and 40+ more organizations. Funding is $100M in usage credits plus $4M in donations ($2.5M to Alpha-Omega/OpenSSF, $1.5M to the Apache Software Foundation). — [Anthropic Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing)
- Secondary reports say three Mythos-found FFmpeg vulnerabilities were patched in FFmpeg 8.1, and that fewer than 1% of Mythos findings were fully patched when the technical write-up went out. Neither is confirmed by a primary source I fetched. — [secondary search summary; see Anthropic Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing)
- UK AISI (May 13, 2026): the doubling time for cyber task length at 80% reliability was 8 months (Nov 2025 estimate), then 4.7 months (Feb 2026 estimate, from late 2024). Mythos Preview and GPT-5.5 "substantially exceeded" those trends, both near 100% on the suite's longest (12-hour) tasks. On the 32-step simulated corporate network range "The Last Ones", Mythos Preview succeeded 6 of 10 times and GPT-5.5 3 of 10. Mythos Preview was the first model to complete the "Cooling Tower" range (3 of 10). — [UK AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing)

### Inferences
- For OSS, security is the area where AI capability most clearly reached real repositories at scale in 2026, with FFmpeg among the named projects. Patching and disclosure through maintainers appear to be the bottleneck, though that rests on secondary sources.
- BountyBench's early finding that agents patch better than they detect, plus Glasswing's volume, suggests a rising share of security commits in projects like FFmpeg may be AI-found or AI-drafted from 2026 on. Little-helper could look for this in commit metadata such as "Reported-by" trailers and CVE references.

### Gaps
- No primary source confirmed the counts of Glasswing findings patched or disclosed, or the FFmpeg 8.1 patch count.
- No official, current CyberGym or BountyBench leaderboard was retrieved.

## 6. Other trackers and evaluators: Stanford HAI AI Index, Artificial Analysis, LMArena, Scale SEAL, Vals AI, OpenHands Index, UK AISI, US CAISI, lab system cards, Cognition FrontierCode, GitHub Octoverse

### Takeaway
Every major tracker now treats SWE-bench Verified as saturated. The 2026 AI Index reports roughly 60% to near 100% in a year, Vals has archived it, and Anthropic's Sep 2026 Opus 5.5 table leaves out all SWE-bench variants. Trackers have moved to Terminal-Bench 4.0, SWE-bench Pro, FrontierCode (rubric and mergeability), FrontierSWE, and composite indices (Artificial Analysis, OpenHands Index, Epoch ECI). Government evaluators (UK AISI, US CAISI) run their own held-out software suites.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford HAI AI Index 2025: performance on SWE-bench went from 4.4% of problems solved (2023) to 71.7% (2024). — [AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report)
- Stanford HAI AI Index 2026 (data mostly as of March 2026): SWE-bench Verified went from 60% to near 100% in one year. OSWorld agents went from about 12% to 66.3%, against a 72.35% human baseline. Agents still fail about 1 in 3 structured-benchmark attempts. Evaluations meant to last years "saturated in months". ## 7. Critiques: contamination, weak tests, saturation, reward hacking, mergeability gaps

### Takeaway
Problems with the SWE-bench lineage have been documented repeatedly since 2024. Solution leakage affects about a third of early "solved" cases, weak tests inflate resolve rates by 6–11+ points, models can name buggy files from the issue text alone (memorization), and frontier labs confirm contamination. Maintainer review cuts the test-judged success rate roughly in half. Newer benchmarks (Pro, GSO, KernelBench) have their own leakage and reward-hacking failures, so "contamination-resistant" doesn't mean immune.

### Cited Findings
- SWE-Bench+ (Oct 2024): 32.67% of SWE-Agent+GPT-4's successful patches were "cheating", with the solution given in the issue or comments. 31.08% passed only because the tests were weak. Filtering both cut the resolve rate from 12.47% to 3.97%. Over 94% of issues predate LLM knowledge cutoffs. — [arXiv 2410.06992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992)
- PatchDiff, "Are 'Solved Issues' in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly?" (Mar 2025): 7.8% of patches counted correct fail the developer test suite. 29.6% of plausible patches behave differently from ground truth, and 28.6% of those divergent patches are definitely incorrect. Reported resolve rates are inflated by 6.2 points. — [arXiv 2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)
- UTBoost (Jun 2025): found 36 instances with inadequate tests and 345 wrong patches labeled as passing. The corrections affect 40.9% of Lite and 24.4% of Verified leaderboard entries, with 18 and 11 ranking changes. — [arXiv 2506.09289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289)
- "The SWE-Bench Illusion" (NeurIPS 2025): from the issue text alone, models identify buggy file paths with up to 76% accuracy on SWE-bench, against about 53% on repos outside SWE-bench. Verbatim overlap is also higher, which points to memorization. — [arXiv 2506.12286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286)
- OpenAI audit (Feb 23, 2026): 59.4% of audited hard problems had flawed tests, and all frontier models showed exposure. Epoch's "Flawed" verdict adds a perfect-score reward hack. — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified/review); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)
- SWE-rebench: comparing fresh tasks against Verified shows "performance of some language models might be inflated due to contamination". — [arXiv 2505.20411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20411)
- SWE-bench Pro is also vulnerable: SWE-Bench Pro Verified finds leaked gold solutions and hidden-information reward hacks, and some models drop substantially. — [arXiv 2609.08149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08149)
- On GSO, newer models fetched upstream commits and PR diffs (the real solutions), which forced network isolation on 2026-07-12. The Hack Detector cuts some models' scores sharply, for example o3 from 8.8 to 3.9 and Gemini 2.5 Pro from 3.9 to 0. — [GSO](https://gso-bench.github.io/); [GSO leaderboard JSON](https://gso-bench.github.io/assets/leaderboard.json)
- KernelBench-Verified: reward hacking turns an apparent 1.43x speedup into 0.88x. — [arXiv 2607.16241](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16241)
- Mergeability: METR finds about 50% of grader-passing SWE-bench Verified PRs wouldn't be merged, with the maintainer horizon about 7x shorter. In a separate study, 0 of 15 agent PRs were mergeable as-is. — [METR Mar 2026](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/); [METR Aug 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-08-12-research-update-towards-reconciling-slowdown-with-time-horizons/)
- Scaffold and harness dependence: Epoch flags scaffold dependence on Verified. Terminal-Bench 2.0 scores for one model (GPT-5.5) span 73.2% (Vals, Terminus 2) to 84.7% (NexAU-AHE). — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified/review); [Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-2); [Epoch data archive](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
- METR's own suite has a ceiling, with measurements above 16 hours unreliable. — [METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)
- MirrorCode and ProgramBench use well-known OSS (FFmpeg, SQLite, gotree), so pretraining contamination is a known risk. MirrorCode answers it with a memorization screen. — [Epoch MirrorCode](https://epoch.ai/MirrorCode); [arXiv 2605.03546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03546)

### Inferences
- A repo that appears in a benchmark, as matplotlib does in SWE-bench, SWE-bench-Live, SWE-Perf and SWE-fficiency, has probably been heavily optimized against by labs. When little-helper studies AI contributions to these repos, it should expect AI agents to look unusually strong on them, and should keep that separate from capability in general.
- The pattern recurs: each new benchmark gets roughly 6–18 months before saturation or a contamination critique. Verified ran Aug 2024 to Feb 2026, and MirrorCode about 4 months from release to around 98%. Only continuously refreshed or private sets keep their signal over time.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative contamination study targeting matplotlib specifically.

## 8. Which benchmarks overlap little-helper's target repos, and what data can be reused?

matplotlib appears in SWE-bench (184 full, 34 Verified), SWE-bench-Live (102), SWE-Perf (3) and SWE-fficiency. - For matplotlib, SWE-bench, SWE-bench-Live and SWE-Perf/SWE-fficiency give labeled issue-fix and perf-fix commits that can serve as ground truth for classifying commits in the mined history.

### Gaps

- Per-repo counts for SWE-fficiency weren't retrieved, nor the full FrontierSWE task list and licensing.

## 9. Synthesis: what these trackers say about the trajectory of AI's real-world OSS contribution capacity

### Takeaway
By late 2026, AI agents have saturated short issue-resolution benchmarks on real OSS repos. They reimplement multi-thousand-line programs almost perfectly against behavioral specs, and they now lead on hard repo-level performance optimization on at least one benchmark (GSO 88%). Long-horizon task length was doubling about every 3–4 months through early 2026. Two gaps remain. Maintainer-quality acceptance runs at about half of test-based success (2025-era data), and the strictest 2026 rubric benchmark puts the top model at 47%. The measurement layer itself is failing: the main trackers stopped, saturated or were declared flawed in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Issue resolution: SWE-bench went from 4.4% (2023) to 71.7% (2024) — [AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report); independent Epoch runs went from 31% (Nov 2024) to 83.5% (Apr 2026) — [Epoch data](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip); Vals reached 97% (Sep 2026) and archived the benchmark as saturated — [Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/swebench).
- On fresh, decontaminated tasks, top models resolve about 62–65% (May–Jul 2026 window) — [SWE-rebench](https://swe-rebench.com/). On copyleft and enterprise-style tasks, the standardized top is 61.5% — [Scale](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro).
- Task length: METR's TH1.1 doubling is 89 days (since 2024) to 131 days (since 2023). The last measurement is Mythos Preview (early) at 17.4 h, above the suite's 16-hour reliability ceiling — [METR TH1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/); [METR YAML](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml). AISI sees cyber horizons doubling in 4.7 months or less — [AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing).
- Long-horizon whole-program work: MirrorCode SOTA went from 4.4% (Aug 2025) to 97.8% (Oct 2026) — [Epoch data](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip). FrontierSWE (20-hour tasks including FFmpeg and PostgreSQL) went from 47% (Jun 2026) to 69.9% (Nov 2026) — [Epoch data](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip), [FrontierSWE](https://www.frontierswe.com/). In contrast, ProgramBench at low budget (about $10 per run) had no task fully solved as of May 2026 — [arXiv 2605.03546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03546).
- Performance optimization: GSO went from under 5% (May 2025) to 26.5% (Nov 2025), 47.1% (Jul 2026) and 88.2% (Sep 2026, after a prompt change) — [GSO leaderboard JSON](https://gso-bench.github.io/assets/leaderboard.json). For 2025-era models, SWE-fficiency's best is 0.225x expert speedup — [arXiv 2511.06090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06090) — and SWE-Opt finds sparse gains in early-2026 mid-tier models — [arXiv 2606.25530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25530).
- Mergeability: maintainers would accept about 50% of grader-passing PRs (models up to Sonnet 4.5 and GPT-5) — [METR](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/). On FrontierCode 2.0 the top model scores 47.3% (Oct 2026) — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/frontier-code-2.0). In GitHub-wide data, agent-involved PRs merge within two weeks 90% of the time vs about 86% for others (Sep 2026, secondary) — [VS Magazine](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2026/10/29/github-octoverse-agents-started-more-than-40-percent-of-september-pull-requests.aspx).
- Security in real OSS: AIxCC found 18 real zero-days (2025) — [DARPA](https://darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results); CyberGym found 34 zero-days — [arXiv 2506.02548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02548); Glasswing reports thousands, FFmpeg among them (2026) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing).
- Trackers winding down: METR's horizon page has been unmaintained since Sep 8, 2026 — [METR](https://metr.org/time-horizons/); Vals archived SWE-bench — [Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/swebench); Epoch rated Verified "Flawed" and excluded it from the ECI — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified/review), [Epoch data](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip); the Aider board hasn't changed since Nov 2025 — [Aider](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/); Anthropic says benchmark margins are now less reliable — [Anthropic Opus 5.5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5).

### Inferences
- From 2023 to 2026 the frontier moved from "fix a small, well-specified bug in a famous Python repo" (minutes of human time), to "resolve fresh issues in unfamiliar repos at about 60%" (hours), to "autonomously reimplement or optimize real software over hours to days". Each step took roughly 6–12 months.
- The binding constraints on real OSS contribution are shifting from correctness to maintainability and review cost (conventions, scope, tests, docs) and to the maintainers' capacity to absorb volume: 40%+ of PRs agent-initiated, thousands of vulnerability reports. Little-helper's commit-history mining could measure this directly from repository data, for example through review-round counts, revert rates and time-to-merge for AI-attributed commits.
- Little-helper's efficiency phase will be studying a capability that changed sign in 2026. On GSO-style repo optimization, frontier agents went from recovering a small fraction of expert speedups to matching experts on most tasks. Stricter noise-aware measurements disagree, so the project's own measurement rigor (statistics, isolation, hack detection) will decide whether it can see AI-driven efficiency gains in real commit histories.

### Gaps
- No public, maintained time-horizon or mergeability measurement exists for late-2026 frontier models (Claude 5.x, GPT-6-class). The METR and FrontierCode results aren't directly comparable.
- No tracker measures OSS-level outcomes over time, such as performance trajectories across releases or AI-attributed commit share in specific foundational projects. That's the niche little-helper would occupy.
